Splitting the Same-Day Patent Value

The KPSS patent value measure divides a firm’s stock-market reaction equally across every patent it is granted that Tuesday. Replacing 1/N with text-similarity weights recovers within-firm-day information that the equal split discards entirely.

Sample 1926–2010 · 1,801,301 patents
Standard errors two-way clustered by firm and grant year

§1The issue

Patents issue on Tuesdays, and a firm typically receives several at once. The median firm-day carries six patents; 81% of patents sit in a firm-day with at least two. The filtered dollar value is identified at the firm-day level, from a single three-day return, so it has to be divided among the patents granted that day. KPSS divides it equally.

Baseline
KPSS 2017 ξj  =  (1 − π̅)−1  1Nj  E[vj | Rj]  Mj
This note ξj  =  (1 − π̅)−1  qjΣg q  E[vj | Rj]  Mj   where  qj = fsim01j / bsim5j

The equal split assigns an identical value to every patent a firm receives on a given day. Any specification identifying off variation among patents of the same firm in the same year therefore has very little left to work with, and the discarded variation is not obviously uninformative.

The weight q is the patent importance ratio of Kelly, Papanikolaou, Seru and Taddy (2021): forward text similarity over the 0–1 year window measured from the filing year, divided by five-year backward similarity. Dating the forward window from filing rather than issue means it closes before the grant date for 80% of patents, so the weight does not use information the market lacked on the announcement day. §5 drops the other 20% outright.

Because the weights sum to one inside every firm-day, the firm-day total is unchanged. This is a reallocation across patents within a firm-day and nothing else. The two measures correlate at 0.9988 in logs; the new weight moves the split by a factor of 0.75 to 1.38 relative to 1/N at the 1st and 99th percentiles.

§2The result in one column

Under the equal split, every patent a firm receives on a given Tuesday is assigned the same value. So in the specification with firm × grant-year fixed effects that also controls for the same-day patent count, the baseline measure has almost no variation left that could track citations.

The coefficient on log Nj of about −0.98 is mechanical, not a finding: since log ξ = log(firm-day value) − log N up to the q tilt, it recovers the identity the measure was built from. Its role is to absorb firm-day scale so the citation coefficient reflects only within-firm-day variation.

§5Robustness

The design rests on fsim01 being known before the grant. Median grant lag is two years, so the 0–1 year forward window normally closes first — but 20% of patents are granted within a year of filing, where it can straddle the grant date. Dropping those patents entirely leaves the result intact.

Inference

The published Table 2 clusters by grant year alone. Because the reweighting operates within firm-day, the firm dimension is the relevant one here, so everything above is two-way clustered by firm and grant year — on the two dimensions separately, following Cameron, Gelbach and Miller. The firm × year term enters only as a fixed effect, never as a cluster dimension. Point estimates are identical across schemes; only the standard errors move.

§6What this does not change

Because the weights sum to one within each firm-day, the firm-day total value is numerically identical under the two weightings. Consequently the firm-year measure Λsmf,t is unchanged — a firm’s patents in a year are a union of its firm-days — and so are the competitor measure ΛsmI\f,t and the aggregate innovation index Λ̂t.

Tables 3 through 6 and Figures 4 and 5 of the published paper are therefore untouched. That is deliberate: the fix is confined to the patent-level allocation, which is the only place the equal split is doing any work. Making the firm-level results respond would require revising the firm-year aggregation as well, which this note does not propose.
